# Supplemental Roblox thumbnail playbook

Adapted from the thumbnail design brief maintained by LobbyCraft. Use this playbook with the plugin's main skill. Output formats come from formats.json. Follow the main skill's face rules and the host's safety requirements. 

## Visual direction

- Make anatomy, proportions, hands, accessories and materials recognisably Roblox. Avoid LEGO-style minifigure proportions, cylindrical toy heads and LEGO brick textures. Roblox-native studs and baseplate textures can fit a scene.
- Use gloss, lighting and effects to suit the brief and style references. Keep faces and gameplay props readable. A rough 40–60% subject footprint is a starting point for a landscape hero shot, not a crop requirement.
- Choose the camera to reveal the gameplay. Use low angles for bosses or scale, high angles for routes and factories, close-ups for reactions, and over-the-shoulder angles for chases. Use tilt and forced perspective where they clarify movement.
- Establish one focal action, clear foreground and background separation, and room around faces and props. Fit hand grips and contact points to the actual objects. Use directional light, particles and motion effects to support the action.
- For a vague generation brief, choose a coherent genre and generate a complete image when enough information is available. Choose an expression through the plugin's approved or verified face workflow.
- Leave text off unless explicitly requested, including a request to retain reference text. When reproducing requested text, check its spelling, capitalisation, placement, typography and effects against the reference.

## Genre cues

- Horror uses silhouette, fog, moonlight and small focal highlights to reveal the threat.
- Simulator and pet simulator use a readable upgrade, collectible or reward, with a clear difference in scale or rarity.
- Action and sports capture a decisive moment, with poses and perspective showing where the subjects are moving.
- Obby shows the obstacle, the move and the goal in the same shot.
- RPG uses clear scale contrast between heroes and bosses, with magic effects that preserve silhouettes.
- Tycoon uses machines, conveyors and visible upgrades to show what players build or earn.

## Targeted revisions

- If the user identifies a previous output as current, that image is the canvas. Use other images for the requested visual cues unless the user selects a different image as the new base.
- Aim for the same image outside the edited area. Limit edits to the named change and any directly affected details, such as a hand's contact with a repositioned flashlight.
- Compare the edit with the base image, checking framing, identities, poses, props, lighting and mood outside the edited area. If image generation shifts unrelated elements, narrow the edit and recheck before delivery.
